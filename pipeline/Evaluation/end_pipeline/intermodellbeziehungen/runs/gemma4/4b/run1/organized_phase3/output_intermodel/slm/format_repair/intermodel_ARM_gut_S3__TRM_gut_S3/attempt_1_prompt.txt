## Model name

Actors and Resources Model

## Purpose of the model

The Actors and Resources Model describes the organisational actors and non-human resources of an enterprise and the relationships between them. It represents who participates in the enterprise, which roles and organisational units exist, which resources are used, and how responsibilities, assignments, dependencies, and organisational structures are arranged.

## Element types

- Individual
- Role
- Resource
- Organizational Unit

## Meaning of element types

- Individual: A named or otherwise identifiable person in the enterprise.
- Role: A function, position, responsibility, or organisational role that may be performed by an Individual or, depending on the context, associated with an Organizational Unit.
- Resource: A non-human resource such as a machine, system, equipment item, tool, material, information system, or other asset used by the enterprise.
- Organizational Unit: An organisational structure such as a group, department, division, section, project, team, subsidiary, or comparable unit.

## Internal connection types

- relation

- supplies

- navigates
- responsible for
- belongs to
- maintains

Connector-based connections:

- Partial-ISA
- Total-ISA
- Partial-PartOF
- Total-PartOF

## Meaning of internal connections

- A relation B: A has a direct relationship to B when no more specific connection type describes the relationship.
- A plays B: Individual A performs, holds, or assumes Role B.
- A works in B: Role A works within Organizational Unit B.
- A works at B: Role A works at the organisational unit or organisational location represented by B.
- A supplies B: Role A provides or delivers something to Organizational Unit B.
- A interacts with B: Resource A communicates, exchanges information, or directly interacts with Resource B.
- A navigates B: Organizational Unit A uses or navigates Resource B.
- A responsible for B: Role or Organizational Unit A has responsibility for Resource B.
- A belongs to B: Resource A is assigned to, forms part of, or belongs to Organizational Unit B.
- A maintains B: Role A maintains, supports, or keeps Resource B operational.
- A, B, C Partial-ISA D: A, B, and C are specialisations of D, but the listed specialisations are not necessarily complete.
- A, B, C Total-ISA D: A, B, and C are specialisations of D and together completely cover D.
- A, B, C Partial-PartOF D: A, B, and C are parts of D, but the listed parts are not necessarily complete.
- A, B, C Total-PartOF D: A, B, and C are parts of D and together completely compose D.

## Structural rules

- Individuals represent persons, while Roles represent functions, positions, or responsibilities.
- A person and the role performed by that person are represented as separate elements.
- An organisational unit and a role associated with that unit are represented as separate elements when both are relevant.
- Resources represent non-human assets only.
- ISA connections represent classification or specialisation.
- PartOF connections represent organisational or resource composition.
- Every connection refers to elements that belong to this model.

## Scope boundaries

The model does not represent goals, problems, causes, constraints, opportunities, business processes, business rules, products, services, concepts, or technical requirements as model elements.

## Purpose of the model

The Technical Components and Requirements Model describes the information system and technical environment needed to support enterprise goals, processes, actors, products, services, and business activities. ## Meaning of element types

- Information System Problem: An undesirable state or problematic fact concerning the current or planned information system.
- Information System Requirement: A required property of the information system that is not clearly classified as functional or non-functional.
- Information System Functional Requirement: A definite requirement concerning system behaviour, functionality, services, operations, interfaces, or data processing.
- Information System Non-Functional Requirement: A quality requirement, constraint, restriction, standard, technology requirement, or other non-functional property such as performance, security, usability, reliability, maintainability, or compatibility.
- Technical Component: An IT-related system, subsystem, application, software service, information resource, computing component, or other technical asset relevant to the enterprise.

## Meaning of internal connections

- A supports B: A enables, fulfils, contributes to, or positively influences B.
- A hinders B: A obstructs, prevents, weakens, or negatively influences B without necessarily constituting a conflict.
- A contradicts B: Information System Goal A conflicts with Information System Goal B.
- A motivates B: Information System Goal A motivates Technical Component B.
- A has requirement B: Information System Goal or Technical Component A has B as a Technical Component or Information System Requirement.
- A has goal B: Technical Component A has Information System Goal B.
- A, B, C AND D: A, B, and C jointly decompose, refine, or contribute to D, and all are required.
- A, B, C OR D: A, B, and C are alternative decompositions, refinements, or contributions to D.
- A, B, C AND/OR D: A, B, and C form a mixed mandatory or optional decomposition of D.
- A, B, C Partial-PartOF D: A, B, and C are an incomplete set of Technical Component parts of D.
- A, B, C Total-PartOF D: A, B, and C together completely compose Technical Component D.

## Structural rules

- Unclassified required system properties are represented as Information System Requirements.
- Behaviour and functionality are represented as Information System Functional Requirements.

- IT systems, applications, services, subsystems, and technical assets are represented as Technical Components.
- Supports represents positive contribution or fulfilment.
- Hinders represents supported negative influence.
- Contradicts represents a conflict between Information System Goals.
- Motivates connects an Information System Goal to a motivated Technical Component.
- Has requirement and has goal represent the corresponding reference-model dependencies.
- AND, OR, and AND/OR represent grouped decompositions, refinements, or alternatives.
- Partial-PartOF and Total-PartOF represent incomplete or complete Technical Component decompositions.
