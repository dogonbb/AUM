Create an Actors and Resources Model according to 4EM for the given case.

## Purpose of the model

The Actors and Resources Model describes how different organisational actors and resources are related to each other.
It shows how actors and resources are related to components of the Goal Model, Business Processes Model, and Business Rule Model.
It explains who is involved, which roles exist, which resources exist, and how responsibilities, dependencies, and organisational relations are structured.

## Allowed element types

Use only the following element types:

- Individual
- Role
- Resource
- Organizational Unit

Do not use any other element types.
Do not create elements such as Goal, Problem, Cause, Constraint, Opportunity, Product, Service, Component, Feature, Concept, Technical Component, Requirement, Process, Market, or Business Rule.

## Allowed direct connection types

Use only the following direct connection types:

- relation
- plays
- works in
- works at
- supplies
- interacts with
- navigates
- responsible for
- belongs to
- maintains

## Allowed connector types

Use only the following connector-based connections:

- Partial-ISA
- Total-ISA
- Partial-PartOF
- Total-PartOF

## Meaning of element types

- Individual: a person in the enterprise.
- Role: a function, position, or responsibility that may be played by Individuals or Organizational Units in different contexts.
- Resource: a non-human resource such as a machine, system, equipment, tool, information system, material, or other resource used in the enterprise.
- Organizational Unit: an organisational structure in the enterprise, such as a group, department, division, section, project, team, subsidiary, or other organisational unit.

## Meaning of direct connections

Use direct connections only for simple one-to-one relations.

- A relation B  
  means that A has a direct relationship to B, when the case states a relationship but no more specific allowed connection type fits.

- A plays B  
  means that A performs, holds, or takes the role B.

- A works in B  
  means that A works within the organizational unit B.

- A works at B  
  means that A works at the organizational unit B or location represented by B.

- A supplies B  
  means that A provides, delivers, or supplies something to B.

- A interacts with B  
  means that A communicates with, exchanges with, or directly interacts with B.

- A navigates B  
  means that A uses or navigates B.

- A responsible for B  
  means that A has responsibility for B.

- A belongs to B  
  means that A is part of, assigned to, or belongs to B.

- A maintains B  
  means that A maintains, supports, or keeps B operational.

## Meaning of connector-based connections

Use connector-based connections when multiple elements are connected by one Partial-ISA, Total-ISA, Partial-PartOF, or Total-PartOF connector.

- A, B, C Partial-ISA D  
  means that A, B, and C are connected to D by a Partial-ISA connector.

- A, B, C Total-ISA D  
  means that A, B, and C are connected to D by a Total-ISA connector.

- A, B, C Partial-PartOF D  
  means that A, B, and C are connected to D by a Partial-PartOF connector.

- A, B, C Total-PartOF D  
  means that A, B, and C are connected to D by a Total-PartOF connector.

## Allowed connection patterns

For relation:

- Individual relation Individual
- Individual relation Role
- Individual relation Resource
- Individual relation Organizational Unit
- Role relation Resource
- Role relation Organizational Unit
- Resource relation Resource
- Resource relation Organizational Unit
- Resource relation Role
- Organizational Unit relation Organizational Unit
- Organizational Unit relation Resource

For plays:

- Individual plays Role

For works in:

- Role works in Organizational Unit

For works at:

- Role works at Organizational Unit

For supplies:

- Role supplies Organizational Unit

For interacts with:

- Resource interacts with Resource

For navigates:

- Organizational Unit navigates Resource

For responsible for:

- Role responsible for Resource
- Organizational Unit responsible for Resource

For belongs to:

- Resource belongs to Organizational Unit

For maintains:

- Role maintains Resource

For Partial-ISA, Total-ISA, Partial-PartOF, and Total-PartOF connectors:

- Individual, Role, Resource, and Organizational Unit may be connected with Individual, Role, Resource, and Organizational Unit.

## Modeling rules

1. Use Individual only for named or identifiable persons in the enterprise.
2. Use Role only for functions, positions, responsibilities, or actor roles.
3. Use Resource only for non-human resources such as machines, systems, equipment, tools, materials, or information systems.
4. Use Organizational Unit only for organisational structures such as groups, departments, divisions, sections, projects, teams, subsidiaries, or similar units.
5. Do not model goals, problems, causes, constraints, opportunities, processes, business rules, products, services, concepts, or technical requirements as elements in this model.
6. If a statement contains both a person and the role the person performs, split it into an Individual and a Role.
7. If a statement contains both an organizational unit and a role played by that unit, split it into an Organizational Unit and a Role.
8. Use plays only when an Individual performs, holds, or takes a Role.
9. Use works in only when a Role works within an Organizational Unit.
10. Use works at only when a Role works at an Organizational Unit or location represented by an Organizational Unit.
11. Use supplies only when a Role provides, delivers, or supplies something to an Organizational Unit.
12. Use interacts with only when two Resources communicate, exchange, or directly interact.
13. Use navigates only when an Organizational Unit uses or navigates a Resource.
14. Use responsible for only when a Role or Organizational Unit has responsibility for a Resource.
15. Use belongs to only when a Resource is part of, assigned to, or belongs to an Organizational Unit.
16. Use maintains only when a Role maintains, supports, or keeps a Resource operational.
17. Use relation only when the case states a direct relationship but no more specific allowed direct connection type fits.
18. Use connector-based connections when the case describes classification, specialization, or part-of structures.
19. Use Partial-ISA when listed elements are specializations or subtypes, but the list is not necessarily complete.
20. Use Total-ISA when listed elements are specializations or subtypes and together completely cover the more general element.
21. Use Partial-PartOF when listed elements are parts of a whole, but the list is not necessarily complete.
22. Use Total-PartOF when listed elements are parts of a whole and together completely compose the whole.
23. Every element used in CONNECTIONS must be listed exactly once in ELEMENTS.
24. Do not invent elements that are not supported by the case text.
25. Do not write explanations, comments, descriptions, bullet points, or any text outside the required output sections.
