## Scenario texts

Our company MultiHeat develops and manufactures heat pumps for family homes. The goal of the company is to provide the most energy-efficient heating solution on the market. The problem that hinders reaching the high-level goal is that the energy consumption of the current product version is too high. This high-level goal can be divided into three sub-goals: sub-goal 1 is to minimize the energy consumption of the heat pump, sub-goal 2 is to produce the remaining energy required by the heat pump with the integrated photovoltaic module, and sub-goal 3 is to provide services supporting high availability of the heat pump. There is a constraint related to sub-goal 2: the maximum energy production of the photovoltaic module is 50 kWh. Sub-goal 1 also can be refined into two sub-goals: sub-goal 11 is to develop a new heat extractor with very low energy consumption, and sub-goal 12 is to re-design the existing heat extractor by adapting it for a new refrigerant. Sub-goal 11 and sub-goal 12 are alternate sub-goals, i.e., only one of them has to be reached to reach sub-goal 1. The problem that hinders sub-goal 11 is the short period available for the development and the problem that hinders sub-goal 12 is that new refrigerants are very expensive. The problems that hinder sub-goal 3 are that the control unit has to be connected to the Internet to provide the status information required for the service and that the company does not have much experience with online information processing. The opportunity connected to sub-goal 3 is a new European regulation for energy-efficient heating that requires continuous monitoring of devices.

Important concepts we have to understand are

## Model name

Goal Model

## Purpose of the model

The Goal Model describes what the enterprise and its employees want to achieve, avoid, or take advantage of. It represents desired states, undesirable states, their causes, external restrictions, and favourable opportunities. The model also explains why processes, rules, requirements, and other enterprise-model elements exist.

## Element types

- Goal
- Problem
- Cause
- Constraint
- Opportunity

## Meaning of element types

- Goal: A desired state, aim, need, requirement, vision, direction, or measurable business objective.
- Problem: An undesirable current or possible future state that hinders the achievement of goals. Threats and weaknesses are represented as Problems.
- Cause: A reason, condition, or situation that produces or explains a Problem or Opportunity.
- Constraint: An external restriction, rule, law, policy, or fixed limitation.
- Opportunity: A favourable situation that the enterprise may want to exploit.

## Internal connection types

Direct connections:

- supports
- hinders
- contradicts
- causes

Connector-based connections:

- AND
- OR
- AND/OR

## Meaning of internal connections

- A supports B: A helps, enables, contributes to, or positively influences B.
- A hinders B: A obstructs, weakens, prevents, or negatively influences B.
- A contradicts B: A is in conflict with B.
- A causes B: A produces, leads to, or explains B.
- A, B, C AND D: A, B, and C jointly contribute to or decompose D, and all are required.
- A, B, C OR D: A, B, and C are alternative contributors to or decompositions of D.
- A, B, C AND/OR D: A, B, and C form a mixed mandatory or optional decomposition of D.

## Structural rules

- Desired states and objectives are represented as Goals.
- Undesirable states, threats, and weaknesses are represented as Problems.
- Reasons for Problems or Opportunities are represented as Causes.
- External restrictions are represented as Constraints.
- Favourable situations are represented as Opportunities.
- Supports represents positive influence.
- Hinders represents negative influence on a Goal.
- Contradicts represents a genuine conflict between Goals.
- Causes connects a Cause to a Problem or Opportunity.
- Connector-based connections represent grouped or decomposed elements.
- Every connection refers to elements that belong to this model.

## Scope boundaries

The model does not represent products, services, components, features, actors, roles, resources, concepts, technical components, requirements, processes, markets, or business rules as model elements.

## Purpose of the model

The Concepts Model defines the things, phenomena, business objects, information objects, and important terms discussed in the other enterprise models. It establishes the business vocabulary of the enterprise, gives precise meanings to relevant expressions, and represents semantic relationships, attributes, classifications, and compositions among concepts.

## Element types

- Attribute

## Meaning of element types

- Concept: A thing, phenomenon, business object, information object, domain object, or important term in the domain of interest that the enterprise needs to reason about, characterise, or define.
- Attribute: A property used only to characterise a Concept.

## Internal connection types

Direct connections:

- 1:1
- 1:n
- n:m
- has attribute

Connector-based connections:

- Partial-ISA
- Total-ISA
- Partial-PartOF
- Total-PartOF

## Meaning of internal connections

- A 1:1 B: One instance of Concept A is related to exactly one instance of Concept B, and vice versa.
- A 1:n B: One instance of Concept A may be related to several instances of Concept B.
- A n:m B: Several instances of Concept A may be related to several instances of Concept B.
- A has attribute B: Concept A is characterised by Attribute B.
- A, B, C Partial-ISA D: A, B, and C are specialisations of D, but the listed specialisations are not necessarily complete.
- A, B, C Total-ISA D: A, B, and C are specialisations of D and together completely cover D.
- A, B, C Partial-PartOF D: A, B, and C are parts of D, but the listed parts are not necessarily complete.
- A, B, C Total-PartOF D: A, B, and C are parts of D and together completely compose D.

## Structural rules

- Concepts represent independent domain terms or objects.
- Attributes represent properties that characterise Concepts and are not treated as independent Concepts when they serve only that purpose.
- Connector-based connections may have one or more sources; a single-source connector is valid.
- Cardinality connections represent semantic associations between Concepts.
- ISA connections represent generalisation and specialisation.
- PartOF connections represent aggregation and composition.
- Every connection refers to elements that belong to this model.

## Scope boundaries

The model does not represent goals, problems, causes, constraints, opportunities, products, services, actors, roles, resources, processes, business rules, or technical requirements as model elements.
