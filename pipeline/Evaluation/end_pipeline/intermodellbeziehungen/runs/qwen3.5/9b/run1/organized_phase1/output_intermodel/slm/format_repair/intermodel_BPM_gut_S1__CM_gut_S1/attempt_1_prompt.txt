## Model name

Business Process Model

## Purpose of the model

The Business Process Model describes enterprise processes, their interactions, and the flow of information between them. It represents internal processes, relevant external processes, information sets, and branching or joining structures. The model explains how information is consumed as input, produced as output, and transferred between process elements.

## Element types

- Process
- External Process
- Information Set

## Meaning of element types

- Process: A collection of activities within the enterprise or problem domain that consumes input, produces output, is controlled by rules, may have a performer or responsible actor, and uses resources and time.
- External Process: A collection of activities outside the organisational scope that communicates with processes in the problem domain and may act as a source or destination of information.
- Information Set: A set of information transferred from one Process or External Process to another.

## Internal connection types

Direct connections:

- relation
- Input
- Output

Connector-based connections:

- Split (AND)
- Join (AND)
- Split (OR)
- Join (OR)

## Meaning of internal connections

- A relation B: A is directly connected to B by an unlabeled business-process relation.
- A Input B: Information Set A is used as input by Process or External Process B.
- A Output B: Process or External Process A produces Information Set B as output.
- A Split (AND) B, C, D: A branches to B, C, and D, and all branches apply.
- A Split (OR) B, C, D: A branches to B, C, and D as alternatives.
- A, B, C Join (AND) D: A, B, and C are joined before D, and all incoming branches apply.
- A, B, C Join (OR) D: A, B, and C are alternative incoming branches joined before D.

## Structural rules

- Internal enterprise activities are represented as Process.
- Activities outside the organisational scope are represented as External Process.
- Transferred information is represented as Information Set.
- Input connections lead from Information Sets to Processes or External Processes.
- Output connections lead from Processes or External Processes to Information Sets.
- AND connectors represent parallel or jointly required branches.
- OR connectors represent alternative branches.
- Every connection refers to elements that belong to this model.

## Scope boundaries

The model does not represent goals, problems, causes, constraints, opportunities, actors, roles, organisational units, products, services, concepts, business rules, or technical requirements as model elements.

## Purpose of the model

The Concepts Model defines the things, phenomena, business objects, information objects, and important terms discussed in the other enterprise models. It establishes the business vocabulary of the enterprise, gives precise meanings to relevant expressions, and represents semantic relationships, attributes, classifications, and compositions among concepts.

## Element types

- Concept
- Attribute

## Meaning of element types

- Concept: A thing, phenomenon, business object, information object, domain object, or important term in the domain of interest that the enterprise needs to reason about, characterise, or define.
- Attribute: A property used only to characterise a Concept.

## Internal connection types

Direct connections:

- 1:1
- 1:n
- n:m
- has attribute

Connector-based connections:

- Partial-ISA
- Total-ISA
- Partial-PartOF
- Total-PartOF

## Meaning of internal connections

- A 1:1 B: One instance of Concept A is related to exactly one instance of Concept B, and vice versa.
- A 1:n B: One instance of Concept A may be related to several instances of Concept B.
- A n:m B: Several instances of Concept A may be related to several instances of Concept B.
- A has attribute B: Concept A is characterised by Attribute B.
- A, B, C Partial-ISA D: A, B, and C are specialisations of D, but the listed specialisations are not necessarily complete.
- A, B, C Total-ISA D: A, B, and C are specialisations of D and together completely cover D.
- A, B, C Partial-PartOF D: A, B, and C are parts of D, but the listed parts are not necessarily complete.
- A, B, C Total-PartOF D: A, B, and C are parts of D and together completely compose D.

## Structural rules

- Concepts represent independent domain terms or objects.
- Attributes represent properties that characterise Concepts and are not treated as independent Concepts when they serve only that purpose.
- Connector-based connections may have one or more sources; a single-source connector is valid.
- Cardinality connections represent semantic associations between Concepts.
- ISA connections represent generalisation and specialisation.
- PartOF connections represent aggregation and composition.
- Every connection refers to elements that belong to this model.

## Scope boundaries

The model does not represent goals, problems, causes, constraints, opportunities, products, services, actors, roles, resources, processes, business rules, or technical requirements as model elements.
